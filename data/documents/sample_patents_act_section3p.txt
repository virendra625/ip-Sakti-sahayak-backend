Chapter II - Inventions Not Patentable: Patentability Exclusions

Section 3: What are not inventions
The following are not inventions within the meaning of this Act:

Section 3(p): Traditional Knowledge Patentability Exclusions and Classical Ayurvedic Formulations
An invention which in effect, is traditional knowledge or which is an aggregation or duplication of known properties of traditionally known component or components.

Explanation:
Under Indian Patent law, pure formulations and classical Ayurvedic medicines (along with Yoga, Unani, Siddha, or Homeopathy) that represent traditional knowledge known to the public through classical authoritative texts or folklore cannot be granted patent monopoly.
However, novel extraction processes, non-obvious combinations demonstrating an unexpected technical effect beyond simple aggregation under Section 3(e), or novel drug delivery systems may be considered provided they satisfy statutory requirements under the Patents Act.

Section 10(4)(d)(ii): Disclosure of Biological Material
Every complete specification must disclose the source and geographical origin of the biological material in the specification, when used in an invention. If the biological resource is obtained from India, prior approval of the National Biodiversity Authority (NBA) is mandatory under Section 6 of the Biological Diversity Act, 2002 before the grant of the patent.
