Chapter II - Inventions Not Patentable

Section 3: What are not inventions
The following are not inventions within the meaning of this Act:

Section 3(p): An invention which in effect, is traditional knowledge or which is an aggregation or duplication of known properties of traditionally known component or components.

Explanation:
Under Indian Patent law, pure formulations of Ayurveda, Yoga, Unani, Siddha, or Homeopathy that represent traditional knowledge known to the public through classical texts or folklore cannot be granted patent monopoly.
However, novel extracts, synergistic chemical combinations demonstrating an unexpected efficacy increase (beyond simple additive properties under Section 3(e)), or novel non-obvious drug delivery systems may be considered provided they overcome Section 3(p) and Section 3(d) hurdles.

Section 10(4)(d)(ii): Disclosure of Biological Material
Every complete specification must disclose the source and geographical origin of the biological material in the specification, when used in an invention. If the biological resource is obtained from India, prior approval of the National Biodiversity Authority (NBA) is mandatory under Section 6 of the Biological Diversity Act, 2002 before the grant of the patent.
